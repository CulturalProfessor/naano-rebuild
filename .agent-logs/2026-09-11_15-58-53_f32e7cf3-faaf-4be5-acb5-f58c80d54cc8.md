Plan is at [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md), 704 lines. The two things you asked for first.

**The load-bearing decision:** naano computes the creator's price before the creator has an opinion, and the brand can only move it inside a fixed band. Step 4 of creator onboarding pre-fills the price under "OUR RECOMMENDATION" with the line "Naano recommends this starting price from the public audience and performance information currently available." The brand's negotiate modal never offers a free number first. It offers 10, 20 or 30 percent off, and only then an "Other" escape. Those are the same decision from both ends, and together they solve cold start. A creator who signed up ninety seconds ago is bookable, because price does not wait for evidence. Its corollary is the dash rule you already flagged, and the two are the same idea: derive what follower count honestly supports, refuse to guess what needs post history.

**The one question:** when the judges say "working product", does a stranger have to hit the live URL, paste their own LinkedIn profile and get a real card, or is signing in as a seeded demo account acceptable? I assumed the second throughout. If it is the first, the real importer has to be production-grade on the demo path and I would want to know before hour two.

Three things I read off the screens that are worth your attention:

- **CPM is derived, not stored.** Post cost divided by median views times 1000 reproduces all four values on the brand shortlist to the euro. Plan revised in place at [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md), now 841 lines. I read your answer as the harder case: a stranger signs up on the live URL with their own LinkedIn profile and gets a real card. Five things changed.

**Auth stops being theatre.** Email and password, hashed, signed cookie, and ownership enforced in the data-access layer rather than in page components. It moves to hour three, because retrofitting scope checks across twenty query sites at hour twenty is how this kind of build dies.

**The importer became a three-tier chain instead of two swappable implementations.** Cache, then your live service, then manual entry. This is the part I am happiest with, because it keeps your original constraint intact rather than trading it away. The 25 seeded profiles sit in the cache tier, so every URL the video touches still resolves locally with no live call. A stranger's own profile misses the cache, hits your service, and gets written back so it is only ever read once. Tier selection is automatic, not an env flag, because a flag would have forced a choice between a camera-safe demo and a working product.

**A cold-start section is new.** A creator who finishes that onboarding and lands in an empty studio has used a card builder, not a marketplace. Seeded open campaigns give them something to apply to on day one.

**The counterparty problem needed a decision, and it is the one thing worth arguing with me about.** Applying is a dead end if no brand is awake, so one seeded brand and two seeded creators respond automatically within about thirty seconds, labelled as such on the row itself. That lets one visitor walk offer to booking to tracked click to payout alone, which is the whole thesis. Labelled it is a demo fixture. **It costs about five hours, and the content approval loop paid for them.** That is now the cut I am least sure about, replacing brand onboarding. It takes the approval checkbox off the best screen on the brand side. There is a one-hour cheap version in the plan if hour 21 is calm.

Section 7 is in [docs/PLAN.md](docs/PLAN.md). The short version: don't pick a theme, port theirs.

**naano is shadcn/ui on Tailwind v4, and their stylesheet proves it.** The `:root` block carries the exact shadcn variable contract, including `--radius: 0.625rem` and `--chart-1` through `--chart-5`. That means matching the recon stops being an eyeballing exercise and becomes a paste. It also doesn't contradict my earlier argument against component libraries, since shadcn copies source into the repo over headless primitives rather than shipping a runtime dependency.

Three findings worth your attention, all read off computed styles rather than guessed:

- **The neutrals are warm, not cool.** Their ink is `#37352f`, which is Notion's, and the surfaces run `#fafaf9` and `#f7f6f3` over an off-white body at `rgb(252,252,251)`. The reflex here is Tailwind `slate`, and slate under that cobalt comes out faintly blue and subtly wrong. Warm greys under a hot blue is the trick.
- **Shadows are blue-tinted and never black**, with heavy negative spread so they tuck under a card instead of haloing it, plus a one-pixel inset white highlight on the lifted surfaces. Hairlines are drawn as a one-pixel box-shadow rather than a border.
- **Two different blues.** The app uses `#2563EB` and the marketing site uses a hotter `#1652f0`. Easy to conflate and it would make the app read slightly off.

Type is Inter at weight 600 to 650 with hard negative tracking, up to -1.71px at 38px. Plus Jakarta Sans is loaded behind a variable and I believe it carries the app's display headlines, though I could not log in to confirm, so that one is marked inferred in the doc. Both are on Google Fonts.

The sky background on the marketplace is two gradients, cloud PNGs and a grain filter. I am reproducing the gradients and the grain and skipping the cloud art and the 3D mascot, because chasing custom illustration is the wrong hour to spend.

This adds no time to the schedule. It moves into the existing hour-zero block, and writing screens against correct tokens from the start is what keeps the polish block free for empty and pending states.

The load-bearing decision is enforced in the type system rather than by discipline. `Metric` is either a known value or an explicit pending, with no third case, so a field we have not measured cannot render as a zero. Four things came out of the build that the plan got wrong or could not have known.

- **Next 16 renamed middleware to `proxy.ts` and ships Cache Components**, which is opt-in. I left it off. Every page here is per-user and database-backed, so partial prerendering would cost Suspense discipline on every route for no benefit.
- **Prisma's `latest` tag currently points at an 8.0 release candidate** with a completely restructured CLI, and it installed against a v7 client. Pinned to the stable 7.10.0 pair. Prisma 7 also moved connection URLs out of the schema into `prisma.config.ts` and now requires a driver adapter.
- **My seeded numbers were inverted and I caught it by checking the output against the recon.** I had median views below follower counts, which produced prices ten times too high and CPMs around €150. On LinkedIn views run well above followers. The recon's four creators sit between 2.7x and 11x.
